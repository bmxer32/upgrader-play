This policy applies only to the Upgrader app and describes all the data the app receives, uses, stores and shares. The Russian version of this page is the original; this is its translation.

What data we process

Advertising — whether or not you are signed in

The app shows rewarded video ads. They are provided by the Yandex advertising network, and its software development kit (SDK) runs from the first launch, even if you have not signed in and have not watched a single video.

If the phone is located in Ukraine, ads in the Android app are provided by Unity LevelPlay mediation (Unity Technologies) instead of Yandex, and everything said below about the ad network applies to it. The app determines the country on the phone itself — from the mobile network, the SIM card or the region in settings — and does not send it anywhere: it is needed only to choose the ad network.

The ad network learns your device's advertising identifier, details about the device and the IP address. We do not decide what this data includes: the network collects it under its own rules in order to show ads. We receive no information about you from it and do not link ad impressions to your game account.

On Android you can reset or delete the advertising identifier in the phone settings, under Privacy → Ads.

Advertising on iPhone

In the iPhone version, videos are shown only by the Yandex advertising network. Its SDK (with Yandex AppMetrica analytics built in) receives device details, ad impression data and the IP address. It receives the advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allowed tracking in the system prompt “Allow this app to track your activity?” — the game asks once. Declining does not limit the game in any way.

You can change the permission at any time: Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

If you play without signing in

Apart from what is described above, we collect nothing. Your progress — balance, items, statistics, upgrade history — is stored only in your device's memory and is not sent anywhere. Deleting the app erases it completely.

There is one exception: a launch counter. Every two minutes the app sends our server a short signal: a random number it makes up at launch, the word “Android”, “iOS” or “browser” and — if you watched ads for a case — how many such cases you have opened since the previous signal. The signal contains nothing else: no device identifier, no advertising identifier, no information about what you do in the game. The random number lives only in the app's memory and disappears with it — the next launch makes up a new one, and two launches cannot be linked to each other.

What we keep from the signal. For the first five minutes the server holds the number in memory to count how many copies of the game are open at once; the result is a record like “thirty people were playing at 21:40”, which contains neither you nor your device. In addition, one row per launch is saved: the same random number, the date, the platform word and how many ad-rewarded cases were opened. It is needed for exactly one thing — to tell “a thousand videos were watched by a thousand people” from “they were watched by forty”; the total number of videos cannot distinguish these two cases. The row is kept for thirty days and then deleted. It cannot identify you, your device or another launch of yours: the random number exists nowhere else and there is nothing to match it against.

As with any internet request, the server sees the sender's IP address: we do not record it in the log of these requests and do not store it anywhere.

If you signed in with Google or Apple

Signing in is optional: the single-player game works fully without it. When you sign in with Google, we receive from Google and store:

  • the account identifier,
  • the e-mail address,
  • the display name,
  • a link to the profile photo.

This data is needed to recognise you at the next launch and to move your progress between devices. The password to your Google account is not passed to the app and is not available to us.

On iPhone you can sign in with Apple instead of Google. The game does not request your name or e-mail address from Apple: Firebase Authentication receives only an identifier of your Apple ID for this app. After that, everything works as with Google sign-in — the same online mode and the same cloud save.

Together with the account data, your game progress is uploaded to the cloud: balance, list of items, win and loss statistics, upgrade history.

If you play in online mode

Signing in with Google or, on iPhone, with Apple opens online mode: duels, friends, the leaderboard. Online mode runs on our own server, which stores a separate player record:

  • the account identifier (the one issued by Firebase),
  • the nickname you chose yourself,
  • balance, list of items and statistics,
  • duel history, friends list and the list of blocked players.

Your e-mail, name and profile photo are not on this server. It knows you by the identifier and the nickname — and by nothing else.

If our server cannot be reached from your network, the app connects to it through a relay server in the European Union. The relay stores no game data and only forwards requests to our server in Moscow over an encrypted connection; like any server, it sees the IP address of the connection and passes it on to our server. This does not change where or how your data is stored.

Your nickname is visible to other players: in the friends list, on the leaderboard, on a challenge card. That is its purpose. Everything else is unavailable to other people — your balance, inventory, e-mail and photo. The nickname can be changed once a month.

Protection against abuse of the invite programme

Both players receive in-game money for an invited friend, and some people try to exploit this: they create a dozen accounts on one phone and enter codes for themselves. To prevent that, when an invite code is entered we store two attributes — and only for this purpose.

Device identifier. In the Android app we read ANDROID_ID — a value the system issues to our app for this device. It never reaches our database in its original form: the server immediately turns it into an irreversible hash with a secret key and stores only the hash. The hash can tell that “a code has already been entered from this device”, but it cannot identify the device or be matched against any other database — ours or anyone else's. We do not read the advertising identifier, the IMEI or the serial number. In a browser, on a computer and in the iPhone app nothing of the kind is collected at all.

IP address. The address of the last sign-in is stored in the player record, and the address from which a code was entered is stored in the invite record. It is needed solely to tell a real friend's invite from two accounts of one person, and is used for nothing else.

Both records are deleted together with the account.

Technical data

Our server, like any server on the internet, sees the IP address of the connection and writes it to the request log. Logs are needed to protect against brute force and abuse and are not used for advertising or tracking.

The Google services that provide sign-in and progress storage record technical details of the connection on their own — including the IP address and device details. They need this to protect against password guessing and abuse.

The app has no analytics of its own: we do not track what you do in the game and do not send it anywhere.

How long we keep data

The data of a signed-in user is kept for as long as their account exists in the app. We do not delete it after a set period and do not archive it: the record lives until you ask for it to be deleted.

On a deletion request we erase the account, the progress document and the online profile on our server — within 30 days. Backups may contain the data for up to 30 more days after that; then they are overwritten automatically.

In the iPhone version there is no waiting: the account is deleted at once, in the game itself — see “Your rights”.

Guest-mode data is not stored on servers at all — it stays only on the device and disappears with the app. The only exception is the anonymous launch counter described above: a row with a random number, the date and the platform is kept for thirty days and is not linked to you or your device.

How we protect data

  • Data travels to the servers over an encrypted connection (TLS) — both Google's and ours.
  • Data in Cloud Firestore is encrypted at rest on Google's side.
  • Database access rules allow only the owner to read and write a document: you cannot see someone else's progress from your account.
  • Every request to our server is verified by the signature of the sign-in token. The app creates no passwords of its own, so there is nothing to steal from us.
  • The app does not request or store the password to your Google or Apple account: sign-in is performed by Google or Apple, and only the result is passed to us.

Permissions

On Android the app itself asks only for internet access and network state.

Along with it, the installation package includes permissions added by the SDKs from Google, Yandex and Unity: the advertising identifier, access to Google Play services, keeping the processor awake while a video loads, installation source details and fingerprint sign-in if your Google account uses it. We do not use these capabilities directly — the SDKs request them themselves.

The device identifier mentioned above is read without any permission: the system issues it to the app itself, and there is nothing to ask for.

In the iPhone version the app asks for one permission — tracking (App Tracking Transparency, see “Advertising on iPhone”). Declining does not limit the game in any way.

The camera, microphone, location, contacts and files are not requested.

Children

The app's age rating is assigned by the store you installed it from and is shown on the app's page there. The app is not designed for children, and we do not knowingly collect their data. If you are a parent and believe that a child has given us data, write to us — we will delete it.

Your rights

  • Play anonymously. Do not sign in, and your game data will not leave the device.
  • Delete your account on iPhone — right in the game. Profile → Settings (gear icon) → “Rights and data” → “Delete account”. After you confirm, the online profile on our server, the copy of your progress in Cloud Firestore and the account in Firebase Authentication are deleted at once; if you signed in with Apple, the permission granted to the app in your Apple ID is revoked as well. You do not need to write to us.
  • Delete the cloud copy and the online profile (Android and browser). Write to us, and we will delete the account, the progress in Firebase and the record on our server. The game will keep working in guest mode. The procedure is described on the account deletion page (in Russian).
  • Delete local data. Deleting the app or clearing its data in Android settings erases all local progress. On iPhone — deleting the app.
  • Reset the advertising identifier. In Android settings, under Privacy → Ads. On iPhone — turn off tracking: Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • Revoke access. You can take away the app's rights to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions, and to your Apple account in Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple.
